Research / Captured November 14, 2026

Current business snapshot.

What the public site says today, what looks strongest, and what Matt needs to confirm before the rebuild begins.

Working evidenceNot final strategySource: public website

Known transition

The current website describes the former joint business.

Matt is expected to become the sole owner of Music Lessons Arizona. Sherin is separating the piano side into her own business, and MLA is not expected to offer piano lessons. Until Matt confirms the details, the live site is evidence of the old model, not a specification for the rebuild.

01 / Company picture

What a visitor sees now

The existing site has strong raw material. Its problem is not a lack of content. It is the job of sorting old and future business facts into one clear story.

01

The public site presents Music Lessons Arizona as a Tempe studio for piano, guitar, bass guitar, and drum set.

02

Its strongest idea is structured musical progress that leads toward collaboration, confidence, and performance.

03

The studio lists 1700 E. Elliot Road, Suites 10 and 11, in Tempe, with nearby students coming from Tempe, Chandler, Mesa, Phoenix, Scottsdale, Ahwatukee, and Gilbert.

04

Some programs also offer online instruction, including a low-latency setup for real-time adult jam sessions.

03 / Strongest signals

The story worth protecting

These are the clearest reasons MLA could matter to a prospective student. Several still need documentation or post-separation permission.

  1. Matt's ASU Jazz Guitar Performance background and work as a multi-instrumentalist, composer, performer, and recording artist
  2. A structured approach that combines technique, theory, real songs, improvisation, and student goals
  3. A path from individual practice into jams, bands, workshops, recording, and public performance
  4. Student performances, testimonials, competition stories, and long-term progress examples
  5. The 2025 BBB Torch Award for Ethics, pending confirmation that future MLA may use it
  6. ESA acceptance, month-to-month tuition, consultation options, and family-friendly logistics, all pending post-separation confirmation

04 / Clarity problems

Where the current site loses the plot

These are working diagnoses, not criticism of the people or teaching. The business appears stronger than the site makes it look.

01

Programs are organized by instrument, age, format, and musical goal at the same time. Visitors have to assemble the offer model themselves.

02

The homepage opens with a generic promise. MLA's specific mix of serious instruction, collaboration, and performance appears much later.

03

Guitar lessons, adult music, kids music, jazz and blues, instrument pages, and jam sessions overlap without showing how they relate.

04

Age and readiness rules conflict across pages.

05

Pricing language varies from starting at $197 per month, to a $150 to $500 range, to pricing only after consultation.

06

The site splits the first action across several forms, calls, and versions of a free consultation.

07

The strongest proof, including credentials, performances, student outcomes, and the BBB award, appears too late.

08

Older brand language remains, including a testimonial that names Tempe Music Studio.

09

Claims such as guaranteed results, three-to-six-month transformation, competition success, and high-school credit need evidence or revision.

10

Seventeen indexed pages and twenty-two indexed posts contain useful material, but the hierarchy makes the business harder to understand.

04

05

Online instruction

Private coaching and real-time collaborative sessions, if Matt wants this to remain distinct.
