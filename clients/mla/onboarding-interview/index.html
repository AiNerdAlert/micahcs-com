How to use this

Follow the business, not the checklist

Capture Matt's exact language when he sounds especially clear or passionate. Follow useful tangents, but mark the type of answer so today's facts do not get mixed with future ideas.

Confirmed decisionFact to verifyBelief or point of viewDesired future stateOpen question

“Before we talk about pages or design, I want to understand the business MLA is becoming. I want the new website to sound like you, explain the right offers clearly, attract the right students, and make the business easier to run.”

Detailed discovery

The full interview guide

Open only the sections the conversation needs. The first section matters most because it controls which assets, claims, and offers we may carry forward.

01
The separation and the new MLAEstablish the business boundary before reusing anything from the current website.
  1. When do you become the sole owner of MLA?
  2. Is the ownership change formalized, or are any business details still being decided?
  3. What will Sherin's new piano business be called?
  4. Will the two businesses share only the location, or also reception, scheduling, staff, equipment, events, marketing, or referrals?
  5. Should the websites acknowledge the relationship, link to each other, or operate independently?
  6. What should current piano students and current MLA students be told, by whom, and when?
  7. Who owns or may continue using the name, domain, phone number, email, logo, website copy, photos, videos, testimonials, social accounts, and mailing lists?
  8. Who controls the Google Business Profile, reviews, Search Console, analytics, advertising accounts, and directory listings?
  9. Does the 2025 BBB Torch Award belong to future MLA, the former joint company, both owners, or a specific legal entity?
  10. Does ESA vendor status remain with MLA, move with Sherin, apply to both, or require a new registration?
  11. Which piano pages should redirect to Sherin, remain as historical content, or be removed?
  12. Is there a date after which the current site would mislead visitors?
02
Why MLA existsFind the belief behind the business before talking about pages or slogans.
  1. Why have you kept teaching and building this business when you could focus only on performing, recording, or another career?
  2. What should music education do for a person besides teach an instrument?
  3. What bothers you about the way music is commonly taught?
  4. What kind of experience do you refuse to give a student?
  5. What moments with students make you feel that this work matters?
  6. Tell me about one student whose change captures why you do this.
  7. What do you want students to believe about themselves after working with you?
  8. What values should guide every MLA decision as the business grows?
  9. If MLA disappeared tomorrow, what would students or the local music community lose?
03
Why the website existsDefine the jobs the site must do for the business and its visitors.
  1. Why is a website important to MLA now?
  2. What should the website do that Instagram, Google, referrals, phone calls, or word of mouth cannot do well enough?
  3. What questions should it answer before someone contacts you?
  4. What should a visitor feel after spending two minutes on it?
  5. What type of visitor should the website encourage to contact you? Who is not a good fit?
  6. What action should be easiest on every page?
  7. What information must you know before agreeing to a consultation?
  8. Should the site help current students, recruit teachers, sell online programs, promote performances, publish educational content, or support partnerships?
  9. Which website tasks would save you the most time each week?
  10. How will we know the site is working?
  11. What would make it a failure even if it looked great?
04
Offers and revenue modelMap the business Matt will actually operate after the separation.
  1. For each offer, what are its name, instrument, age range, skill level, format, session length, schedule, capacity, price, and expected result?
  2. Which offers run every week, and which are occasional, seasonal, experimental, or invitation-only?
  3. Which offer is easiest to explain and sell?
  4. Which offer is most profitable? Which produces the strongest student results?
  5. Which offer do you most want to grow?
  6. Are there programs you want to stop, combine, rename, or create?
  7. Will songwriting, theory, composition, production, recording, or performance coaching be separate offers or parts of lessons?
  8. Is online instruction a supporting option or a business you want to grow beyond Arizona?
  9. Do you want a membership, course, workshop, camp, band, masterclass, or performance program later?
05
Students and buyersIdentify the people MLA serves best and the person who makes the buying decision.
  1. Who usually makes the decision and pays: the student, parent, spouse, school, ESA account holder, or someone else?
  2. Describe your best current teen student. Describe your best current adult student.
  3. Are complete beginners a strong fit, or do you prefer students with experience?
  4. What ages are genuinely a good fit for guitar, bass, and drums?
  5. What level of practice and commitment do you expect?
  6. What goals bring people to you?
  7. What has usually failed before they contact you?
  8. What are they afraid of or embarrassed about?
  9. What objections stop them from enrolling?
  10. Which inquiries repeatedly turn out to be a poor fit?
  11. How far will people travel to the Tempe studio, and which local cities matter most?
  12. Is there a different ideal customer for online instruction?
06
Method and differenceTurn Matt's teaching approach into something specific and supportable.
  1. How do you assess a new student's level and goals?
  2. How do you decide what to teach first?
  3. How much is structured curriculum versus adapting to a student's favorite music?
  4. How do theory, technique, ear training, improvisation, rhythm, and repertoire fit together?
  5. How do you prevent bad habits and show progress?
  6. How do students learn to play with other musicians?
  7. What does your song-based approach look like in a real lesson?
  8. What can MLA offer that a good independent teacher, a large franchise, YouTube, or an app cannot?
  9. Which parts of your background matter most to students?
  10. What does the current website overstate, understate, or misunderstand about your real difference?
07
Student journey and conversionSee the path from first discovery through long-term participation.
  1. How do most people hear about MLA today?
  2. What happens from discovery through the first paid session?
  3. Who responds to inquiries, how quickly, and what do they collect first?
  4. Do you offer a call, Zoom meeting, in-person assessment, trial lesson, or class observation?
  5. What makes a consultation convert well?
  6. Where do good leads disappear or stop responding?
  7. What questions are asked almost every time?
  8. What happens during the first 30, 60, and 90 days?
  9. How do students move from private lessons into groups, jams, bands, recording, or performance?
  10. What should the website automate, and what should remain a personal conversation?
08
Staff and operationsFind what the site and supporting systems need to make easier.
  1. Who works for MLA now, and who will remain after the separation?
  2. Who handles inquiries, enrollment, scheduling, billing, reminders, and customer service?
  3. What systems do you use for scheduling, payments, email, text, forms, files, and lesson materials?
  4. Where is the same information entered more than once?
  5. What manual work do you wish would disappear?
  6. What breaks when you get busy?
  7. What are your teaching and room-capacity limits?
  8. Are there days, times, instruments, or programs with unused capacity?
  9. Do you want to hire more instructors? What makes someone a fit?
  10. What policy, business risk, or operational headache worries you most?
09
Problems and prioritiesSeparate the urgent business needs from work that can wait.
  1. What are the three biggest problems in the business today?
  2. Which problem costs the most time, money, or stress?
  3. Which problem hurts the student experience most?
  4. What does the current website make harder?
  5. What do people misunderstand before contacting you?
  6. What work are you doing because the website fails to do it?
  7. What have you already tried?
  8. What must improve in the next 90 days? What can wait?
10
Brand and voiceDefine how MLA should feel and how Matt naturally explains it.
  1. What does the name Music Lessons Arizona mean to you now?
  2. Should the public lead with Music Lessons Arizona, MLA, your own name, or a combination?
  3. Should MLA feel like a studio, school, community, coaching practice, artist-development program, or something else?
  4. Which three words should describe the brand? Which three should never describe it?
  5. How prominent should you personally be on the website?
  6. Which artists, venues, album covers, studios, or music brands match your taste?
  7. What do you like and dislike about the current red, black, and white identity?
  8. Does the current logo still fit? What visual clichés should we avoid?
  9. Should the site speak differently to parents, teens, adult beginners, and experienced musicians?
  10. What phrases do you use naturally that the current site does not?
11
Proof and trustSeparate useful evidence from claims that need support or permission.
  1. Which credentials and professional experiences matter most to prospective students?
  2. Which student stories best prove the MLA method?
  3. Which testimonials belong to future MLA and may be republished?
  4. Which photos and videos have clear permission for future use?
  5. Which performances, recordings, bands, or student releases can we show?
  6. Which claims can be documented?
  7. What guarantees or outcome promises are safe and honest?
  8. Are there before-and-after recordings or progress milestones?
  9. Which reviews appear on Google, Facebook, BBB, or other platforms?
  10. Can MLA ask current students for new post-separation testimonials?
12
Marketing and growthChoose the growth paths Matt can support, not every possible channel.
  1. Where do the best current students come from?
  2. Which sources produce inquiries but poor-fit students?
  3. How important are Google search and Google Maps?
  4. Which searches do you think people use to find you?
  5. Which competitors or alternatives do prospects compare you with?
  6. What partnerships produce leads?
  7. Which social platforms are worth maintaining?
  8. What content could you produce consistently without it becoming a burden?
  9. Are there events, workshops, performances, or offers that should generate leads?
  10. Do you want growth through local enrollment, online work, staff, group programs, or products?
  11. What does a healthy roster and program mix look like in one year?
13
Content, accounts, and accessIdentify the material and systems required before the rebuild can move safely.
  1. Who owns the domain and hosting account?
  2. Who can edit the current WordPress site?
  3. Who owns the logo files, photos, videos, and written curriculum?
  4. Which analytics, search, advertising, social, email, scheduling, payment, and CRM accounts exist?
  5. Which accounts are shared with Sherin and need to be separated?
  6. Where are customer and lead records stored?
  7. Which forms receive inquiries, and where do they go?
  8. What privacy, consent, minor-student, media-release, payment, and data-retention rules apply?
  9. Which pages currently rank or receive leads and need a redirect plan?
  10. Who can approve facts, pricing, policies, brand decisions, and launch?
14
Scope, timing, and successDefine a realistic first release and the decisions that control it.
  1. Is there a public date for the ownership and piano separation?
  2. When should the new MLA site launch?
  3. What must be correct on day one? What can launch later?
  4. Are there budget, time, staffing, content, or technology limits?
  5. Are there tools or platforms that must stay?
  6. What would you rather simplify than rebuild?
  7. Who reviews the work and gives final approval?
  8. How quickly can factual questions and content approvals be answered?
  9. What are the three most important outcomes for this project?
  10. What did I not ask that I should understand before rebuilding MLA?

The stopping point

Do not jump from this interview straight into design.

First, turn Matt's answers into a confirmed business brief. The website should express those decisions, not make them for us.
